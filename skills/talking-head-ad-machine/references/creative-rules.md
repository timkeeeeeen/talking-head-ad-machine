# Creative rules

## Editorial priority

1. Truth and preserved meaning.
2. Hook strength.
3. Clarity and proof.
4. Natural delivery.
5. Pacing.
6. Visual polish.

Build the spoken audio spine before graphics. Prefer a complete, emotionally specific idea over a shorter contextless line.

## Default structure

- Hook, `0–2s`: use a result, conflict, question, demonstration, or contrarian statement already supported by the recording.
- Problem: name a recognizable situation.
- Mechanism: explain what changes and why.
- Proof: show supplied evidence, product pixels, or an accurately framed demonstration.
- Objection: handle the likely objection only when supported.
- CTA: give one clear next action.

Create alternate hooks around a shared body before multiplying bodies, styles, and CTAs. This keeps tests interpretable.

## Cut policy

- Remove abandoned attempts when a complete attempt exists.
- Remove fillers only when the splice remains natural.
- Compress long pauses without removing all breath.
- Keep connectors that preserve grammar, causality, contrast, and sequence.
- Never stitch incomplete fragments into a synthetic sentence.
- Never reorder statements so their implication changes.
- Mark uncertain semantic cuts below `0.8` confidence for review.

## Visual policy

Use one primary visual idea at a time. Captions support accessibility and retention; graphics add meaning rather than repeat every word.

Prefer first-frame hook type, supplied product screens, one-number callouts, old/new comparisons, short process diagrams, proof annotations, and a restrained CTA card.

Avoid generic stock-office imagery, fake dashboards, decorative charts, constant zooms, excessive sound effects, and overlays covering the face or captions.

## Claims

Never convert an aspiration into a result, a personal experience into a universal promise, or correlation into causation. Preserve qualifiers. Flag health, financial, employment, housing, and other sensitive claims for explicit review.
